# Validation 31 — Orchestration, abstraction, and narrative resolution — player as lens

**Status:** APPROVED CONCEPTUALLY



## Decisions and contracts

- The world has history, causality, goals, and dynamics independent of the protagonist.
- The player acts as a lens that determines which parts receive greater narrative and contextual resolution; the player does not control the global simulation.
- Global, regional, local, and immediate resolutions are different levels of the same reality.
- The lens retrieves established facts, infers compatible details, develops abstract elements, and records details according to canonization policy.
- Creative development cannot contradict canon, chronology, geography, epistemology, causality, resources, or rules.
- Global simulation continues: if the protagonist abandons a mission to study, a patrol may destroy the signal pyre and advance the antagonist’s plan, provided conditions and events permit it.
- Actions and consequences outside the protagonist’s presence continue and become known only through valid means.
- Narrative should be immersive, literary, and paced: experiential density, not word volume. Avoid simplistic responses and purposeless prolixity.
- Adaptive resolution does not replace prior contracts; it is transversal to all of them.


## Implementation rule
Implementations must preserve these decisions and their cross-system contracts. Technical difficulties must be recorded as conflicts/proposals, never resolved through silent relaxation.
